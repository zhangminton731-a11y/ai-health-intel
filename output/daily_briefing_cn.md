Silent evaluation reveals context-dependent validity of automated metrics for AI-generated clinical messages
Evaluation of AI-generated patient communication lacks standardized frameworks, and automated metrics may not reflect clinical utility. We leveraged a staged rollout of GPT-generated draft responses (GDRs) to patient portal messages. Thirty primary care physicians received drafts from September 2023 to August 2024 under two conditions: an intervention group where AI drafts were displayed and edited (n = 21,073), and a non-displayed group where drafts were generated but never shown to physicians, who composed responses independently (n = 45,127). We evaluated messages using BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, and Flesch Reading Ease, and then correlated automated metrics with blinded physician ratings of effectiveness, misunderstanding potential, and harm. In the intervention group, 2264 (11%) drafts were incorporated into final messages with high semantic alignment (BERTScore F1 = 0.93).

Multi-site real-world performance of commercial AI for pulmonary and incidental pulmonary embolism detection
Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a leading cause of cardiovascular mortality, yet the real-world performance of FDA-cleared AI detection models remains incompletely characterized. Reference-standard labels were extracted from radiology reports using a validated LLM pipeline (97% accuracy, κ = 0.94). The PE model achieved 86.8% sensitivity and 99.1% specificity, with sensitivity declining from 99.3% for saddle emboli to 72.9% for subsegmental PE, and from 89.7% for acute to 65.3% for non-acute PE. The iPE model achieved 73.5% sensitivity and 99.8% specificity. Both models demonstrated lower sensitivity than FDA-clearance benchmarks while exceeding cleared specificity, with diminishing performance for peripheral and non-acute emboli mirroring known human reader limitations and underscoring the need for standardized post-market surveillance of AI-enabled medical devices.

Global, regional and national burden of pertussis in 1990–2023: a Global Burden of Disease study
Despite long-standing immunization programs, pertussis remains a major cause of vaccine-preventable mortality, particularly among infants. Here we present a primary assessment of global pertussis incidence, mortality and disability-adjusted life years across 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023. We show that despite short-term pandemic-period fluctuations, the dominant global pattern from 1990 to 2023 was long-term decline: age-standardized mortality decreased by 61.2% and age-standardized incidence by 41.6%. Relative to the anomalously low pandemic-period estimates, incidence and mortality rebounded by 102.6% (95% uncertainty interval, 77.0–128.2%) and 66.1% (95% uncertainty interval, 2.4–174.5%), respectively, between 2020 and 2023. In 2023 alone, we estimated 18.9 million cases and 115,000 deaths attributable to pertussis.
